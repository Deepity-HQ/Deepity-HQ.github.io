Detailed Description

Google Highway (github.com/google/highway) runtime-dispatched implementations for several CPUBackend elementwise kernels that were previously plain scalar loops: AddBiasPerChannel, Fill, MultiplyInto, and (for a subset of ActivationType, see below) FusedActivationDerivativeMultiply, FusedStateUpdate and FusedStateUpdateMomentum. See CPUBackendHighway.h for why this is its own CMake target, compiled without the project's -march pin.

dGELU is never vectorized in FusedActivationDerivativeMultiply, FusedStateUpdate or FusedStateUpdateMomentum: its derivative needs Sleef_tanhf_u10, a transcendental function, and wiring SLEEF's own vectorized tanh into a Highway lane loop was a real, separate piece of work, not rushed into those kernels' own pass. dSIGMOID and dTANH are vectorized in FusedActivationDerivativeMultiply (its "from an already- activated value" closed form needs no transcendental: sig*(1-sig), 1-tanh^2) but NOT in FusedStateUpdate/FusedStateUpdateMomentum (their dType is evaluated from the raw pre-activation z, which for dSIGMOID and dTANH needs std::exp / Sleef_tanhf_u10 respectively – see ActivationDerivativeScalar in Activations.h). All three transcendental cases stay on CPUBackend.cpp's existing scalar path for whichever of those three kernels needs them.

FusedActivationDerivativeMultiplyFromPreact, below, is the exception: it needs the derivative from the raw pre-activation value for EVERY ActivationType (it's SimplePCLayer/SimpleConvPCLayer's correctness- fixed convention, see those layers' own UpdateState()/UpdateWeights()), so skipping dGELU/dSIGMOID/dTANH isn't an option. It uses hwy/contrib/math/math-inl.h's own portable, vectorized Tanh instead of SLEEF – a few ULP looser (see that header's documented precision tiers), but genuinely SIMD, unlike a scalar std::exp/std::tanh fallback loop (measured: a scalar fallback here was slower overall than the two separate, already-vectorized calls it replaced, because it threw away the 8-16x SIMD width SLEEF already got on the transcendental call – this kernel is what closes that gap properly instead).

Deliberately Tanh, not Exp: math-inl.h's Exp is only valid up to float32 +104 (see its own doc comment) with no guardrail past that – dSIGMOID's derivative is written here via the exact identity sigmoid'(z) = 0.25*(1 - tanh(z/2)^2) specifically to route through Tanh's own internal domain-safety clamp instead (it short-circuits to +-1 past |x| > ~18.7, because ITS internal Expm1 call isn't safe past that either). A direct hn::Exp(d, hn::Neg(z)) call silently produced garbage once a layer's pre-activation legitimately drifted past -104 during real training, which this file's own kernel verification test (random inputs in [-1.5, 1.5]) had no way to catch – confirmed by a full MNIST run NaN-ing out around epoch 10 before this fix.
